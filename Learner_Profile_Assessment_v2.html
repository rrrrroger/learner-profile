Learner Profile: How Do You Learn Best?

About 20 minutes. No grade, no right answers.

Before You Start

Most people don't learn just one way. This is a way to notice how you tend to like to work, so you can make choices about how you study next semester.

You'll answer 28 short questions. For each one, pick the answer that sounds most like you on a normal day, even if two of them fit. Go with your first instinct.

Nothing you pick is saved or sent anywhere. The results stay on your screen.

How It Works

  • Part 1-4: seven questions each, about 3-4 minutes a part
  • Your results: your top two profiles, plus a score for all seven
  • Reflection: three questions to think about once you see your results

An Honest Note About "Learning Styles"

These profiles describe what you prefer. They are not a label, and they are not a box you have to stay in.

Researchers who reviewed the evidence found no adequate support for the idea that studying in the format that matches your style helps you learn more. What does seem to help most people is mixing methods and testing yourself, like quizzing from memory, explaining ideas in your own words, and spreading your studying over several days.

So use your results to notice what feels natural, then try a method from a profile you scored low on. That's where a lot of the growth is.

Try This Next Semester

  • Pick one strategy from your top profile and use it for a week.
  • Pick one strategy from a profile you scored low on and try it too.
  • After a test or assignment, ask yourself which method actually helped. Your results are a starting guess, and your own experience is the better evidence.

Reflect

  1. Did anything in your results surprise you? Does it sound like how you actually studied this semester?
  2. Think of a class that went well and one that didn't. Did you study differently in each one?
  3. What is one change you'll make to how you study in the spring?

Where these come from: the Visual, Auditory, Reading/Writing, and Hands-On profiles follow the VARK model (Fleming and Mills, 1992). Social, Solitary, and Logical are often added to make a seven-profile list. The idea that hands-on learning works best when you also reflect on it comes from David Kolb's experiential learning cycle. The note on research is based on Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork (2008), "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence."
